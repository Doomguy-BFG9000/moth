The Moth and the Lamp

There was once a moth who lived in the garden of a house by the sea. Every night the moth flew to the
window of the house, because in the window there was a lamp, and the lamp was the brightest thing the
moth had ever seen.

The other moths said: the lamp is too hot, and the glass is too hard, and the light is not for you. But
the moth did not listen. Every night it flew to the window and beat its wings against the glass, and
every morning it went back to the garden, tired and a little burnt, and slept until the sun went down.

One night the window was open. The moth flew in, and round the lamp, and round it again, closer and
closer, until it could see that the light was not a thing at all but a little flame inside a glass, and
that the flame was smaller than the moth.

The moth sat on the edge of the lamp and thought about this for a long time. Then it flew out of the
window, back to the garden, and told the other moths what it had seen. They did not believe it. So the
next night the moth took them all to the window, and the window was open, and they saw it too.

After that the moths of the garden were not afraid of the lamp, and they were not in love with it
either. They knew what it was. And on the darkest nights, when the sea was loud, they would sit on the
window and watch the little flame, and the moth who had seen it first would tell the story again.
